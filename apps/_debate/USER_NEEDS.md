# What the person actually struggles with (in their words, then translated)

> "I struggle with completing daily tasks, also like cleaning the basement, even in sections; completing social media
> projects, 3D design projects, work at SprintRay projects; making time for family stuff without feeling like it's
> affecting my work productivity; a clean disconnect so that I can focus on personal vs work. And yes, budgeting and
> all of those values are all great as well."

They work at SprintRay (a dental 3D-printing company), do 3D design and social media projects on the side or for work,
and run a household with family. The email on the account looks like a shop/business account (orders, suppliers).

## Needs, mapped to Workbench

1. **Big physical jobs, done in sections** (the basement). Photograph the whole space once; it becomes a *map of zones*
   (e.g. "shelf wall", "under stairs", "bike corner"). Each session = one zone, one short mission with pins, an after
   photo verifies it, and the map fills in. Progress across weeks is visible and honest (ghost line = the pace they chose).
2. **Creative and work projects that stall** (social media series, 3D design projects, SprintRay work projects).
   A *project instrument*: a live sentence like "Launch reel series: [4] of [12] posts done, at [2] a week you finish
   [Nov 22]." The hinge is usually the weekly pace or the size of the next step. Committing produces the one next
   concrete step (a 25-minute focus block as an .ics, a Drive brief/outline, a Gmail draft to a collaborator), never a
   wall of to-dos. A photo of a sketch, a print on the build plate, a whiteboard or a screen can feed it.
3. **Family time without guilt about work** — a *week instrument*: "Work [42] h with [3] deep-focus blocks still ships
   your [SprintRay] commitments; that leaves [9] family evenings / [2] protected weekend mornings." Dragging family
   time up shows what (if anything) it actually costs — usually less than feared — and where to protect focus instead.
4. **A clean disconnect between work and personal** — two modes, **Work** and **Home**, each with its own instruments.
   A deliberate **Clock out** ritual (~60 seconds): capture open work loops into tomorrow's first step so the brain can
   let go, then the work instruments go dark/locked until the next **Clock in**, and Home opens. The app itself
   respects the boundary (no work content shown in Home mode).
5. **Budgeting and recurring costs** — already covered by the leak / per-use molds; keep them prominent.
6. **Daily tasks** — the same rule as everything else: one prepared next step per instrument, finishable today.

Keep the panel's principles: honest numbers (seen / inferred / assumed / set), one hinge, one real act per commit,
sessions that end, no streaks or guilt.
